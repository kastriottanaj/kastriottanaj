problems:
  - title: "The portal owns your buyer"
    detail: "Listings sit on MerrJep, Gjirafa, Idealista or ImmoScout, so the portal ranks, the portal gets the enquiry, and it also shows the buyer four competing agencies on the same screen. The moment you stop paying, the pipeline stops."
  - title: "Sold listings take their rankings with them"
    detail: "A property page earns links and impressions for months, then gets deleted the day it sells. Most agency sites bleed authority this way every single month and never see it, because nobody measures a URL that no longer exists."
  - title: "Filters eat the crawl budget"
    detail: "Price, bedrooms, area and status combine into tens of thousands of near-identical filtered URLs. Google spends its visits there instead of on the pages you actually want indexed — which is how a site ends up with eleven pages in the index and no idea why."
  - title: "Enquiries go cold before anyone calls"
    detail: "Property is a speed-to-lead business. A form that lands in a shared inbox and gets read after lunch competes against an agent who replied on WhatsApp in four minutes — and loses, however good the listing was."

faq:
  - q: "Can SEO actually beat the property portals?"
    a: "Not on a bare query like \"apartments for sale\" — portals have a decade of authority and thousands of listings behind them, and that fight is not worth funding. What an agency does win is the longer, more specific search: a named neighbourhood, a development, a budget, a buyer profile, a question about the process. That traffic is smaller and converts far better, because someone typing it has already narrowed down what they want. AS Real Estate's average position of 6.8 is that principle working."
  - q: "What should happen to a listing page when the property sells?"
    a: "Almost never a plain delete. Depending on how much the page has earned, it either stays up marked as sold — which is useful proof that you move property in that area — or redirects to the development, building or neighbourhood page it belongs to. Deleting it throws away every link and every ranking signal it accumulated while it was live."
  - q: "We get our listings from a feed. Is that a problem?"
    a: "The feed itself is fine. The problem is publishing the developer's description verbatim, exactly as forty other agencies publish it, and letting the feed generate a URL structure nobody designed. The fix is usually a thin layer of your own on top: original descriptions on the properties that matter, a sane URL and canonical scheme, and control over what gets indexed."
  - q: "Do you work with developers as well as agencies?"
    a: "Yes, and the work differs. A developer is marketing one project with a finite number of units and a hard deadline, so the site is closer to a campaign — the development is the page, and the job is visibility plus a lead system that holds up during launch. An agency needs the durable version: an inventory that keeps changing on top of location pages that don't."
  - q: "How long did the AS Real Estate results take?"
    a: "The Search Console window on this page is three months, and the indexing jump lands at the start of July — roughly five weeks in. That is the usual shape: technical fixes move first because they unblock everything else, then impressions build, then clicks follow. The Business Profile numbers run from March, because local visibility was the part that could start earning before the site was fixed."
