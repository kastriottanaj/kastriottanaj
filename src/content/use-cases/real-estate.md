## Why this market is different

Most industries have a search problem or a follow-up problem. Real estate reliably has
both, and they compound: the portals absorb the demand at the top, and whatever trickles
through to your own site arrives as a form submission that sits unread while a competing
agent is already on the phone.

The portal dependency is the part agencies feel and the part they treat as fixed. It
isn't, but it is also not solved by trying to outrank a portal on its own terms. A portal
wins on inventory and authority. An agency wins on specificity — the neighbourhood it
actually knows, the developments it actually sells, the questions a first-time buyer in
that city actually asks before they ever open a listing.

## The structural problem nobody measures

The technical half of this is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: **a real estate
site destroys its own SEO on a schedule.** Every sold property is a URL that earned
impressions, gathered a few links, and then vanished. Multiply by the number of
properties you move in a year and you have a site that resets its own progress
continuously, while the agency wonders why rankings never seem to build.

Meanwhile the filtered views — every combination of price band, bedroom count, area and
status — generate more URLs in a week than the site has real pages. Google crawls them,
finds them nearly identical, and gets slower about crawling anything on the domain.

Neither problem shows up in a rankings report. Both show up immediately in a crawl.
