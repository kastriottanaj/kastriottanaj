  - title: "The search has a town in it, and Google answers it with a map"
    detail: "Nobody types \"Tiefbau\". They type \"Tiefbau Remscheid\" or \"Bagger mieten in der Nähe\", on a phone, often standing on the plot. Google answers that with three businesses on a map, and the blue links underneath are mostly directories. A firm without a Business Profile — or with one that was set up once and never touched — is not in the race, however good the work is."
  - title: "Nine services on one page, so none of them rank"
    detail: "The typical trade site is a home page, a phone number and a gallery of finished patios. Paving, pool construction, excavator hire and winter service are four different customers with four different searches, and one page listing all of them cannot be the answer to any of them."
  - title: "The lead platforms own the enquiry"
    detail: "MyHammer, Check24 and the directories rank for \"Gartenbau Remscheid\" ahead of the businesses in Remscheid, sell the same lead to three firms at once and price it up as you come to depend on it. Like the property portals: the moment you stop paying, the pipeline stops."
  - title: "The quote goes to whoever answered"
    detail: "A tradesperson takes calls on site, with one hand on a machine. The call that goes to voicemail at ten is a job that went to the next firm on the map by lunch, and a contact form read that evening competes with a WhatsApp answered in four minutes."
plays:
  - title: "A website built around the searches, not the org chart"
    detail: "I built atbau-nrw.de from the ground up: a home page that says what the firm does and where, in the first line; a services page that names each trade — Tiefbau, paving and patios, garden and landscape construction, fences, earthworks, demolition, pools and ponds — in the words a customer uses; and a phone number, opening hours and address on every page, matching the Business Profile to the letter."
  - title: "A page for the service that carries its own demand"
    detail: "Excavator hire is its own search — \"Bagger mieten Remscheid\" — so it got its own page, titled the way the customer types it, with the mini and compact machines listed, the with-or-without-operator question answered, and the things people actually ask before they book: can I drive it myself, how short-notice, what are the safety rules. Winter service the same."
  - title: "The Business Profile as the front door"
    detail: "Category, services, hours, photos of real jobs and a 5.0 rating from 24 reviews. For April to mid-September 2026 the profile's own reporting shows 344 interactions: 229 direction requests, 103 clicks through to the site and 8 calls tapped straight from the listing."
  - title: "Markup that ties the site to the map"
    detail: "LocalBusiness structured data carrying the street address, coordinates, phone and opening hours, matching the profile to the letter — so Google can tie the website to the listing, and the listing to the pin, without guessing which AT Bau is which."
  - title: "Pages written for the towns you drive to"
    detail: "The map pack is drawn around the searcher, so a firm in Remscheid fades with every kilometre towards Wuppertal, Solingen and Wermelskirchen. Service-area pages hold the edge when they are written for real — the jobs done there, the routes — rather than one page per town with a swapped name."

faq:
  - q: "Do we need a website, or is the Google Business Profile enough?"
    a: "The profile does the first job on its own — the map position, the call button, the directions — and for a one-person firm it is where to start. But a profile ranks on what it can prove, and the website is the proof: a page for each service, photographs of the jobs, the towns you cover in writing. AT Bau's 103 website clicks from the listing are people who wanted more than a rating and a phone number before they called."
  - q: "Which towns can a trade business actually rank in?"
    a: "Google's radius, not yours. The map pack is drawn around the person searching, so a firm in Remscheid ranks in Remscheid and the towns it borders — Wuppertal, Solingen, Wermelskirchen, Radevormwald — and fades with distance. A page for a town helps at the edge of that circle when it says something true about the work done there. A page per town with the same text and a swapped name is the thing Google learned to ignore years ago."
  - q: "Should we pay MyHammer or Check24 for leads?"
    a: "When the diary is empty, yes, as a bridge — a paid lead today beats an organic one in six months. The mistake is treating the platform as the channel rather than the bridge. It sells the same lead to three firms, raises the price as you come to depend on it, and keeps the reviews you earn there. Everything built on your own profile and site stays yours when you stop paying."
  - q: "How much do reviews affect ranking?"
    a: "Count, recency and words, in that order. A profile with 24 reviews at 5.0 outranks one with six at 4.8 in the same town, and a review that names the service — the excavator, the paving, the town — tells Google what to rank you for. The mechanism is asking, the same day the job finishes, with a link. The discipline is that most firms stop asking after the first ten."
  - q: "The trade is seasonal. Does that show in the numbers?"
    a: "Yes, and it is worth reading properly. AT Bau's profile peaked in May, when gardens get built, dipped in July and recovered in August as the autumn jobs were booked — that shape is the trade, not the search work, and the September point on this page is half a month. The answer to it is a counter-seasonal service with its own page, so the site has something to rank for in the months the excavator sits. AT Bau's is winter service."
